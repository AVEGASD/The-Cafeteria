The question for this one

In the context of money, politics, religion, power, and family — what do you actually care about?

When I was younger, the cafeteria was the first place I remember having truly deep conversations. We were young. We had no answers. We were thinking together, openly, trying to figure things out.

It didn't matter what the topic was. What mattered was we were talking and thinking with one another.

Thousands of years ago the Greeks and Romans had a version of this. They called it the agora — where philosophers, business owners, professionals, and common folk came together to discuss the issues of their time.

Today we have The Cafeteria.

How an evening works

  1. You're seated in a small groupSix people to a table, with a lead whose job is to start the conversation and keep it balanced — not to referee it.
  2. One question opens the nightEveryone gets the same question. Where it goes from there is up to the table.
  3. After the first round, groups rotateNew table, new people, same question from a different angle. The second round is the table's to shape.
  4. We close togetherEveryone back in one room. One thing you heard that you're still thinking about.

The only rules

  1. Go at ideas, not people.
  2. Let people finish.
  3. If you feel yourself getting heated, that's fine. You just don't get to lose control of it.

What we've found so far

Every attendee fills out a short survey before and after. Two events in, the numbers say something worth paying attention to.

5.0Felt respected, even when their ideas were challenged
4.7Felt heard
9/9Left with a view shifted or better understood
9/9Said they'd come back

The honest caveat: nobody in the room has identified as conservative yet. That's the limit on anything we can claim right now, and it's the thing this next one is built to change. If you disagree with the people who invited you, you're exactly who should be there.
